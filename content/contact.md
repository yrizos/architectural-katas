We started running [architectural katas](/what-is-a-kata/) at [Epignosis](https://www.epignosishq.com/) as its [Architecture Modernization Enabling Team](https://medium.com/nick-tune-tech-strategy-blog/architecture-modernization-enabling-teams-amet-23a7f867b284). We were after what Gregor Hohpe calls [architecture without architects](https://architectelevator.com/architecture/organizing-architecture/), where teams own their architecture and anyone on the team can play architect for a while when a big decision comes up.

Hohpe also points out the catch. The model only works for teams with an architectural mindset and decision discipline, and neither of those shows up because someone sent a memo. People build them by designing systems, again and again.

Real projects rarely offer that many chances, so that's what our katas are for. Everyone on our teams gets far more design practice on made-up problems, and nothing they try there can break production.

Technical leadership has the same catch. Habits like explaining a blocker to someone outside engineering or stating what a decision costs don't show up because of a memo either. You build them through [short exercises for everyday technical leadership](https://leadership-katas.yrizos.com/), which take 10 to 15 minutes of deliberate practice whenever one of those moments comes up at work.

If any of this sounds familiar, feel free to reach out. We can happily talk architecture for hours 😉
