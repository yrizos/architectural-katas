# The Workshop

## Purpose

This page explains how we run the workshop, both inside Epignosis and at tech events. It describes our format.

The reader leaves knowing what happens in a session, how the tech event version differs from the internal one, and what a team takes away.

## Key Information

- Who it is for. Anyone, whatever their role or experience. That includes a developer new to architecture, a tech lead who wants decisions challenged, and an architect after more reps.
- Facilitators. Aggelos Bellos and Yannis Rizos.
- Format. Teams of three to five people. Inside Epignosis a session runs two hours. At a tech event the conference slot cuts it to one hour. Both figures come from the current About page.
- Each team gets a realistic brief, makes the design calls, and defends them when the room pushes back. When another team solves the same problem differently, the reader sees the assumptions they did not know they were making.
- The session follows three phases, Understand and Prioritize, Design, and Share. This page states no timings per phase and no roles beyond the facilitators.
- What teams leave with. Their top characteristics, decisions with alternatives and trade-offs, a diagram, and a release plan.
- Inside Epignosis the teams already work together. At a tech event most people at a table have only just met, so the team builds trust while it designs. The room mixes far more specialties and experience levels. The slot is half as long, so teams prioritize faster and leave more questions open. The About page is the source.
- Ted Neward's rules already ask co-workers not to share a group. At a tech event that happens without anyone arranging it.
- The workshop builds on Ted Neward's rules and moderator guide at architecturalkatas.com.
