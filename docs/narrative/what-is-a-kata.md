# What Is a Kata

## Purpose

This page explains what an architectural kata is. It is plain and educational, and it is written for a reader who has never heard of one.

The reader leaves knowing what a kata asks of them, why it is useful, and where it comes from. The page does not teach the steps. How to Participate does that.

## Key Information

- An architectural kata is a practice exercise in software design. The reader gets a short brief for a system that does not exist yet and works out an architecture that could serve it.
- A session asks the reader to decide which requirements matter most, to question what the brief leaves out, to sketch the main components and how they fit together, and to explain why the design beats the alternatives.
- Most engineers design a system from scratch only a few times in their career. A kata gives them that practice far more often, and nothing touches production.
- There is no correct answer, only trade-offs. Working with others is the point, because a design nobody questions teaches little.
- Origin. The idea comes from the martial arts kata, then Ted Neward's architectural katas. These claims need checking against architecturalkatas.com before they ship.
- A brief has a description, the users, and numbered requirements, and it leaves deliberate gaps. The example is "The Show Must Go On!", a platform for an annual technology conference.
- The brief facts to preserve are the early-bird ticket sales date, the archive of previous editions, and the five requirements (Conference Management, Attendee Engagement, Conference Archive, Mobile Accessibility, Reliability).
