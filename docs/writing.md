# Writing

Scope: site copy in `content/` and `layouts/`. On overlap, the stricter rule wins.

## Voice and Meaning

- Write for technically literate professionals. Assume expertise.
- Sound like an experienced colleague explaining something at work. Use familiar words and concrete reasoning.
- Speak directly to the reader. Do not narrate what the site or its pages contain.
- Lead with the important point and keep the context needed to understand it. Brevity is not clarity.
- Be encouraging and light. Warmth comes from concrete scenes and honest reassurance, not from exclamation marks, praise, or jokes that need explaining.
- Avoid marketing, slogans, jargon, filler, artificial contrasts, and language that makes ordinary points sound profound.
- Distinguish facts, assumptions, interpretations, and recommendations. Source claims about people or external sites from material read during writing.

## Flow and Readability

- Keep paragraphs short and focused on one point. Connect each paragraph to the previous one through its content.
- Vary sentence length naturally. Avoid overloaded sentences and chains of punchy, disconnected statements.
- Do not impose sentence, paragraph, or word-count targets.
- Remove repetition unless it serves a clear purpose.
- Use descriptive headings in APA title case. Use prose for explanations, bullets for independent parallel items, and numbered lists for steps.
- End a sentence that introduces a list with a colon.
- Keep list items and questions complete. Do not use a label or question followed by a colon and an explanation.

## Mechanics and Boundaries

- Use US English, the serial comma, and straight quotes. Avoid regional idioms.
- No em dashes, ellipses, or dashes or semicolons joining clauses. Use parentheses only when they affect meaning.
- Use digits for precise figures and words for casual counts. Use articles and complete grammar.
- Use bold sparingly, never for an entire sentence. No decorative horizontal rules, footnotes, or muted notes.
- Reserve backticks for identifiers, commands, paths, and literal values. Give code fences a language and do not hard-wrap prose.
- No outreach calls to action. Event pages have no ticket, registration, or "join us" calls to action.
- Preserve existing tone and intentional choices. Do not manufacture edits to good writing.
