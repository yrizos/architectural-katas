About the Workshop

Most engineers design a system from scratch only a few times in their career. Ted Neward created Architectural Katas so they could do it far more often.

Each team gets a realistic brief, makes the design calls and defends them when the room pushes back. When another team solves the same problem differently, you start to see the assumptions you didn’t know you were making.

We run katas at Epignosis to give our engineers that experience away from production. Whether you’re a developer new to architecture, a tech lead who wants your decisions challenged or an architect after more reps, you’ll get something out of today.

The Kata

Open Conf 2026

The Show Must Go On!

Description

A team of organizers runs a large annual technology conference, bringing together thousands of developers, technology professionals, and industry experts.

Each year features a new lineup of speakers, talks, and activities. The organizers need a platform to manage the conference, keep attendees informed, and support interactions between speakers and participants.

Early-bird ticket sales open on February 21, 2027.

The platform must also maintain an archive of previous editions, allowing visitors to explore past schedules, speakers, and presentations.

Users

Thousandsof attendees
Hundredsof speakers
Dozensof organizers and event staff

Requirements

  1. Conference Management

    Organizers and speakers can manage talks, schedules, room assignments, and conference information.

  2. Attendee Engagement

    Attendees can explore the program, interact with talks, provide feedback, and receive important updates.

  3. Conference Archive

    Visitors can explore previous editions, including their programs, speakers, and presentations.

  4. Mobile Accessibility

    All capabilities must be usable on mobile devices, including during the conference.

  5. Reliability

    The platform must remain useful during periods of high demand and unreliable venue connectivity.

Cheat Sheet

Open Conf 2026

Understand and Prioritize

List the actors and what they do, write down assumptions, pick the top three characteristics, and decide what must ship first and what can wait.

Design

Group actions into components, decide what to build and what to buy, choose a style, draw the architecture, and note each key decision on the diagram with the trade‑off it accepts.

Share

Present your top characteristics, diagram, release plan, and one key trade‑off.

Architectural Characteristics

  • Availability
  • Scalability
  • Elasticity
  • Performance
  • Security
  • Maintainability
  • Testability
  • Deployability
  • Interoperability
  • Cost

Architectural Styles

  • Layered
  • Modular monolith
  • Service-based
  • Microservices
  • Event-driven
  • Microkernel

Product Decisions

  • Build or buy
  • Off-the-shelf, open source, or custom
  • Managed service or self-hosted
  • Extend an existing system or replace it

Phasing

First releaseThe smallest scope that delivers value to the main actor.
Later releasesEverything else, ordered by value.

Time is a factor. The date you must ship by limits what fits in the first release.

Ground Rules

  • There is no correct answer, only trade‑offs.
  • No technology names until the shape is clear.
  • An unstated assumption is a hidden decision.
